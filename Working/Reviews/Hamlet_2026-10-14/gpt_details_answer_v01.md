## 3. Ten layout rules

1. **Preserve the anchor arrangement.** Church northwest; base immediately south; barn north-centre; field 1 northeast; shop central; tower east; well and pond west; field 2 southwest; most housing southeast.

2. **Build six loose clusters of 4–7 houses.** Put roughly two-thirds of homes south/east; use the remainder around base, the church approach and the barn lane.

3. **Vary setbacks between 3–7 m.** Face most entrances toward a lane; turn occasional houses sideways. Follow local road bends rather than random rotation.

4. **Keep 4–8 m between house walls.** Occasionally widen to 10–12 m for a shared orchard, work area or shortcut. Avoid identical gaps.

5. **Imply ownership through use.** Garden beds, washing lines, worn paths and differently maintained grass suggest plots. Leave frequent passages; avoid continuous hedge boundaries.

6. **Give each home 1–3 substantial additions.** Choose combinations by household: poultry keeper, gardener, mechanic, commuter, elderly resident. Some homes share sheds or summer kitchens.

7. **Separate roadside and rear activity.** Porches, benches, flowers and mailboxes face lanes; sheds, compost, wood and vegetable beds sit beside or behind houses. Put carports where vehicles can reach them.

8. **Reserve purposeful open spaces.** Shop forecourt about 12 × 15 m; church gathering space 10 × 12 m; barn loading apron 10 × 15 m. Surround each with activity and shade.

9. **Provide a drivable loop and chase shortcuts.** Start with 4.5–5 m main lanes, 3–3.5 m minor lanes and 1.5–2 m pedestrian gaps. Verify bends and pickup areas using the actual van and dragging mechanics.

10. **Place detail through cause.** Mud follows drainage; shade attracts benches; repairs collect beside workshops. Reach the river east of the asphalt through a legible crossing and bank path. Use quiet stretches between dense activity pockets.

## 4. Five story spots

- **Grandpa’s repair bench:** half-fixed appliances and an awkward grape arbor turn returning an NPC into domestic chaos.
- **Watermelon stall:** a fleeing shopkeeper circles stacked crates while loose melons roll into the van’s approach.
- **Tractor repair bay:** players split around the tractor as an NPC escapes through the barn’s second doorway.
- **River net rack:** an NPC ducks behind hanging nets, then doubles back around the beached skiff.
- **Wedding platform:** faded decorations frame a conspicuously empty dance floor—the perfect public stage for a disastrous capture.
