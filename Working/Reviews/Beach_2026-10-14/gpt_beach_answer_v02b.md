## 5. Dumb treasure hunter — 3 options

### T1 — Bottle-Cap Tsar

Pitch: A treasure hunter who has found nothing but bottle caps, yet proudly wears them like royal jewels.

- Hook: An enormous crown assembled from oversized, rusty beer-bottle caps. Six jagged cap-spikes form a ridiculous royal silhouette.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Spiky crown / sawtooth head. Crown approximately 60 cm wide, projecting beyond his head on both sides.
- Face: 75% Happy / 25% Surprised. One fake-gold tooth, permanently smug grin, vacant crossed eyes, scruffy moustache.
- Palette: Rust orange, tarnished silver, dirty navy; cheap-gold accent.
- Hand: Metal detector, dropped on danger. Worn: Bottle-cap crown, chin strap.
- Gag: He drops his detector and hides, but the crown spikes remain visible above beach furniture. When dragged into the van, he resembles a dethroned king of rubbish.
- CIS reference: «Искал золото — накопал пивных крышек». The beach detector enthusiast whose entire collection is worthless.
- Build: Low. Six exaggerated cap meshes, crown base, straps. Approximately 450–800 new triangles, excluding the shared detector.

Weakness: At distance, it could read as an eccentric king rather than a treasure hunter once the detector is dropped.

### T2 — The Walking Sieve

Best pick

Pitch: A beach treasure hunter who has literally turned himself into a giant sand-sifting machine.

- Hook: An absurdly large orange metal sand sieve strapped diagonally across his stomach and hip, like a massive perforated shield.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Huge tilted circular disc. Approximately 80 cm in diameter, deep and angled outward so its rim breaks the body outline from front, side and rear. Use a thick rim and a few enormous perforations, not fine mesh.
- Face: 65% Surprised / 35% Happy. Cross-eyed stare, protruding front teeth, patchy moustache, sand-covered eyebrows.
- Palette: Tangerine orange (dominant), weathered steel, beige; turquoise accent.
- Hand: Long metal detector with circular search coil, dropped on danger. Worn: Giant sieve, diagonal shoulder harness, small detector headphones.
- Gag: He notices players, drops his valuable detector and runs away wearing the completely useless sieve. When grabbed and dragged, the oversized sieve makes his ragdoll resemble a discarded kitchen appliance.
- CIS reference: «Пляжный кладоискатель», hunting for lost wedding rings and gold chains, while finding bottle caps and small change.
- Build: Low-medium. Thick shallow sieve bowl, large cutout holes, shoulder harness, headphones. Approximately 600–1,100 triangles, plus 300–500 for a shared metal detector.

Why it wins: A single absurdly oversized piece of actual treasure-hunting equipment creates a recognisable circular silhouette. It is more grounded in the beach setting than a cartoon crown or magnetic gadget.

### T3 — The Gold Magnet Genius

Pitch: He believes a metal detector works better if he also wears a giant magnet to attract the gold.

- Hook: A massive horseshoe magnet worn as a backpack, with two coloured poles extending above his shoulders.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Open U-shape, about 75 cm wide. A thick, curved magnet with two tall prongs framing the head.
- Face: 80% Happy / 20% Surprised. Large stupid grin, single bucktooth, uneven eyebrows, slightly misaligned pupils.
- Palette: Bright teal (dominant), charcoal, ivory; orange pole-tip accents.
- Hand: Metal detector, dropped on danger. Worn: Huge horseshoe magnet and backpack harness.
- Gag: He drops his actual detector and flees while carrying the giant fake magnet. The U-shaped frame remains visible when he's hiding or lying in the van. No real magnetic force needs implementing.
- CIS reference: «А золото-то магнитится?» — the confidently stupid amateur who doesn't understand what his equipment detects.
- Build: Low. Thick low-poly horseshoe, pole tips, harness. Approximately 450–800 triangles, plus the shared detector.

Animation note: All three use the existing idle, panic, hide and ragdoll animations. The metal detector is one rigid hand prop. A believable side-to-side searching sweep would require one additional animation loop, but none of these concepts depends on it.

## 6. Dumb ordinary holidaymaker — 3 options

The plain T-shirt and shorts must remain clearly recognisable as the base NPC. The joke should come from one excessive mistake, not a completely new costume.

### H1 — Room-Key Hostage

Best pick

Pitch: A man so afraid of losing his hotel room key that he has turned it into a gigantic piece of wearable luggage.

- Hook: An enormous old-fashioned hotel key fob and attached brass key, worn diagonally on a heavy lanyard. The fob is approximately 80–90 cm long, like a wooden paddle, with a giant room number.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Asymmetric hanging pendulum / paddle. A long chunky object extends from the shoulder toward one knee, jutting beyond one hip. The oversized key teeth make its lower end distinctive.
- Face: 60% Happy / 40% Surprised. Crossed eyes, two stupidly large teeth, blank smile, one eyebrow permanently raised.
- Palette: Violet purple T-shirt (dominant), beige shorts, light wood; brass-gold accent.
- Hand: Half-eaten ice cream, dropped on danger. Worn: Giant wooden key fob, brass key and diagonal securing strap.
- Gag: He drops his ice cream and panic-runs, still wearing the absurdly oversized hotel key. When dragged into the van, the wooden fob protrudes from his body like a luggage tag.
- CIS reference: Old hotel and sanatorium keys with ridiculously bulky numbered wooden or plastic fobs. «Главное — ключ от номера не потерять!»
- Build: Very low. One oversized bevelled wooden fob, key, ring and rigid strap. Approximately 300–600 triangles.

Why it wins: It keeps almost the entire base NPC intact while introducing one huge, recognisable piece of everyday CIS holiday stupidity. The diagonal shape also avoids competing with the other five silhouettes.

### H2 — Buffet Sphere

Pitch: An all-inclusive tourist who has made it his life's mission to eat back the entire cost of his holiday.

- Hook: An enormously distended spherical belly stretching his otherwise ordinary T-shirt into a near-perfect ball.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Round bowling-ball torso with ordinary-sized head and skinny legs. Belly slider maximum; body scale 0.9–0.95 to exaggerate proportions.
- Face: 85% Happy / 15% Blink. Greasy grin, food crumbs near his mouth, half-closed vacant eyes, protruding tooth.
- Palette: Deep lavender (dominant), beige, charcoal; tomato-red accent.
- Hand: Tiny overloaded buffet plate, dropped on danger. Worn: Plain stretched T-shirt, hotel all-inclusive wristband.
- Gag: He drops his precious food immediately when frightened. Players then have to drag his oversized round torso through the van doorway.
- CIS reference: «Шведский стол — надо отбить стоимость путёвки!» The all-inclusive tourist trying to eat everything because it's free.
- Build: Very low. Mostly belly slider and adjusted shirt deformation; plate and small food shapes approximately 150–400 triangles.

Weakness: The belly communicates overeating better than stupidity. Without the plate, it risks becoming an ordinary overweight tourist.

### H3 — The Lost Map Genius

Pitch: A tourist who cannot find the beach even though he's standing on it, and has converted his resort map into a hat.

- Hook: A gigantic folded paper resort map worn upside down as a peaked roof over his head.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Wide angular paper roof, overhanging both sides of the head. Triangular paper folds rather than a normal panama or sunhat.
- Face: 80% Surprised / 20% Happy. Confused crossed eyes, long buckteeth, tiny open mouth, eyebrows partly hidden by the paper brim.
- Palette: Hot magenta T-shirt (dominant), off-white map, grey shorts; lime accent for a huge map arrow.
- Hand: Cheap plastic souvenir cup, dropped on danger. Worn: Giant folded map-hat with chin strap.
- Gag: He drops his cup and runs. His oversized paper map remains perched over his head, even while hiding or being transported unconscious.
- CIS reference: The package tourist asking «А где тут пляж?» while already standing on the beach; navigating an obvious location with a map.
- Build: Very low. A few thick folded paper planes, printed map texture, chin strap. Approximately 200–450 triangles.

Weakness: The visual reads as an absurd paper hat first and a map second. It needs sufficiently exaggerated map graphics to sell the joke.

## 7. Sunburnt tourist — SPF bottle vs sour-cream lobster

I would replace the SPF bottle. Vadim's sour-cream idea is better.

### B1 — Giant unopened SPF bottle

Strengths: Strong external silhouette, unmistakable product shape, excellent visibility from above, persistent rigid prop after ragdoll.

Weaknesses: Why has a tourist strapped an enormous sunscreen bottle to his chest? It's a deliberately invented visual gag rather than an immediately recognisable person. The unopened detail is too small to communicate at 40 m. The bottle also hides part of the sunburn.

### B4 — Sour-Cream Lobster

New final pick

Pitch: A catastrophically sunburnt tourist trying to repair himself with a traditional folk remedy.

- Hook: Tomato-red skin with a huge, sharply defined white vest-shaped tan line, plus thick sour-cream smears across both shoulders and nose.
- Silhouette at 40 m: Long, narrow, arms-splayed lobster. Thin slider, scale 1.1, idle arm angle approximately 48–50° so his arms visibly hang away from his painful torso.
- Face: 80% Worried / 20% Surprised. Miserable eyes, clenched crooked teeth, red nose partly covered with sour cream.
- Palette: Lobster red (dominant), chalk white, faded navy; turquoise accent on the jar label.
- Hand: Small sour-cream jar, dropped on danger. Worn: Navy swimming trunks only. Tan line and sour cream are part of the body materials.
- Gag: He drops his folk remedy and panic-runs with his arms absurdly far from his body. On the lounger, he resembles a badly painted red-and-white sausage. Being dragged turns him into a miserable, stiff-looking lobster despite his floppy ragdoll.
- CIS reference: «Обгорел на море — помажься сметаной!» Instantly familiar folk-remedy behaviour.
- Build: Very low. Baked skin masks, optional raised sour-cream blobs, simple jar. Approximately 150–300 new triangles.

### The lounger test

Sour-cream lobster — overhead

From above: The sour-cream version wins on character recognition. The white vest-shaped tan line occupies much of the torso and is visible even when the jar has dropped.

From the side: The SPF bottle has a stronger physical silhouette. The sour-cream version needs white shoulder patches wrapping around the upper torso, bright-red exposed limbs and a distinctly pained expression.

Verdict: Use B4 Sour-Cream Lobster. It has slightly weaker geometry-based recognition than the giant bottle, but a much better CIS-specific joke and stronger connection between appearance and behaviour. No new animation is necessary.

## 8. Rebalancing the complete six-character set

Only one of the four previous winners needs to change: Replace the SPF bottle with the sour-cream lobster. Keep the corn tower, castle mould and inflatable lifeguard biceps.

The final shape hierarchy is:

- Tall: Corn seller — tall vertical corn tower.
- Low: Sandcastle builder — squat, squared-off fortress.
- Long / splayed: Sunburnt tourist — thin red body and separated arms.
- Wide / top-heavy: Lifeguard — massive inflatable upper-body triangle.
- Circular: Treasure hunter — enormous tilted sieve disc.
- Diagonal / asymmetric: Ordinary holidaymaker — oversized hotel-key pendulum.

For the treasure hunter, position the sieve diagonally and give it a deep rim; a thin flat disc would disappear edge-on. For the holidaymaker, place the key fob off-centre with its end projecting beyond one hip, but leave most of the plain purple T-shirt visible.

For all six, use simple accessory collision shapes and keep the giant props rigidly attached to existing bones. The comedy should survive dragging and van loading without requiring secondary simulations or new animation states.
