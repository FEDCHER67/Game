# Map refinement specification

Date: 13 October 2026. Revision: v01.

Refine the existing VOLUNTEERS ONLY map so that it feels compact, inhabited and shaped by terrain from the player's viewpoint. The main problems are excessive spacing, flat ground, empty areas, long unintended views and gaps exposing unfinished surroundings. Buildings also need convincing depth and collision on surfaces the player can reach.

The decisions below were settled with Fedya through the map specification interview. They define the work to implement and review. They do not certify the current map or an implemented revision.

All Markdown specifications, briefs and reports for this work must be written in English. Discussion with Fedya remains in Russian.

It also updates the earlier expectation that the van can drive almost everywhere through meadows and light forest: roads should normally be faster and more dependable, while off-road travel remains possible in selected places.

## Layout and scale

### Allowed changes

Compact individual districts and reduce excessive spacing between them while preserving their relative arrangement, the main route connections, the coastline and the river. Internal streets, building groups, plots and district extents may change to achieve this.

Buildings, bases and points of interest may move within their own district. Preserve each point's purpose, usable access and route connections. Move associated entrances, yards, loading areas and access paths together when needed. Update road and pedestrian graphs to match the revised positions.

The Construction to Downtown to Elite Hill connection may gain bends and offsets. Preserve its connectivity and usable vehicle passage while interrupting its long straight perspective. The v18 report identifies an approximately 593 m straight road sequence here; use the current scene to locate the actual views needing correction.

### Human scale

Use compact buildings with proportions that remain credible beside the player, NPCs and van. Reduce oversized masses, yards and gaps where they make the map feel stretched. Keep entrances usable for carrying an NPC and vehicle access usable for the actual van.

The inherited ArtDirection.md reference sizes are a 1.75 m human, 2.1 to 2.4 m doors and approximately 2.8 m storeys. Check these against the current characters and assets. They are existing scale references, not evidence that every present building has the correct proportions.

Do not apply a uniform world scale multiplier. Retain the physical size of the characters and van while adjusting the environment around them. Do not adopt a blanket percentage reduction for all districts: choose changes by the problem visible in each place.

Before changing a problem area, record its building dimensions, key gaps and route distances. Compare the revised area from the same camera positions and with the same vehicle settings. Keep useful courtyards, loading areas, road clearance and purposeful open spaces.

## Terrain and travel outside roads

### Terrain composition

Replace broad featureless ground with terrain that has local shape and larger spatial divisions. The agreed starting ranges are:

| Terrain role | Typical vertical change | Intended result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small undulations, raised plots and shallow local depressions | 0.3 to 1.5 m | Break flat ground and support convincing placement. |
| Separating hills, hollows and embankments | 3 to 8 m | Divide spaces, interrupt unwanted views and influence off-road routes. |
| Existing major hills, mountain and boundary ridge | Existing site-specific heights | Preserve their distinct role; review separately from the ranges above. |

These are composition ranges, not a requirement to place a mound at regular intervals. Use slopes, ditches, hollows, embankments and rock outcrops where they suit the site. Fit buildings, paths, road shoulders and water edges to the terrain. Avoid floating objects, buried entrances, ground cracks and abrupt changes at district boundaries.

Use relatively calm surfaces where activities need them: loading, parking, courtyards, pedestrian access and work areas. Nature and terrain should connect districts without exposing rectangular clearings or an artificial border around each district.

### Roads and off-road routes

Roads should normally be the fastest, most dependable way to travel between districts. Outside them, terrain and substantial natural obstacles should make driving across the ground inconvenient and sometimes impossible. Retain selected readable passages rather than enclosing every district completely. Pedestrians should generally have more passage opportunities than the van.

Vehicle upgrades may make some off-road passages easier. The required map result is defined first for the ordinary van; exact upgrade effects remain a separate vehicle tuning decision. This specification does not define upgrade statistics or guaranteed new routes.

Keep main roads comfortable for the actual van, including curves, transitions and access points. The v18 report records road grades at or below 8 percent; retain this as the inherited road grade check when revising terrain. Driving validation must also cover local bumps, grounding, tipping and turning, which the grade figure alone cannot establish.

For every pilot, identify the intended off-road passages and the directions deliberately obstructed by terrain or solid objects. Compare a normal road trip with the corresponding off-road attempt using the same van configuration. Record travel time, detours and physical difficulties. Revise areas where cutting across empty ground is consistently easier than using the road.

## Views and unfinished gaps

Alternate enclosed or partly enclosed spaces with deliberate openings. Buildings, relief and vegetation should normally give the view a composed endpoint. Natural areas may have broader views if terrain and vegetation give those views content.

Retain selected views towards the sea, a landmark or from a height. Make their direction and purpose explicit during layout review. Do not close every panorama or convert the entire map into narrow corridors.

Address both kinds of unwanted exposure:

- Long views across empty space or through districts, especially along ordinary travel routes.
- Gaps between or beneath objects, terrain seams and boundary openings that expose unfinished ground or the outside of the map.

Use coherent building placement, terrain and planting to compose views. Fog and small scattered props should not be the main remedy for a large empty space or a broken boundary.

Evaluate views from the player's walking camera, the player's view while travelling in the van, and reachable raised surfaces. Top-down density and the report's 2D sightline estimates are supporting information; acceptance depends on the actual game camera.

## Nature and ground surfaces

Nature should fit the game's existing restrained stylized look. Use consistent colours, silhouette complexity and material treatment so grass, shrubs and stones blend with the rest of the scene. Preserve the current art direction without treating an unaccepted palette candidate as newly approved.

Place nature unevenly: grass patches, exposed ground, stones, shrubs and groups of trees with meaningful spaces between them. Increase density where it supports a district transition, the roadside or an inconvenient off-road cut. Keep paths, entrances, loading areas and work surfaces readable and usable.

Use terrain shape and vegetation together. A random scatter of additional trees over a flat plane does not fulfil the terrain requirement. Preserve the existing rule for irregular tree placement rather than turning all planting into regular street rows.

Blend suitable ground surfaces such as dirt, grass, gravel and sand. Add wheel tracks, worn paths and selective wear where use explains them. Avoid evenly distributed noise or a different material style for every district.

Tree trunks, large rocks and logs are solid obstacles. Grass and soft shrubs are traversable. Match solid collision to the visible form closely enough that passages and obstructions are understandable. Small decorative stones may remain surface detail rather than vehicle obstacles.

## Buildings and nearby detail

Use a level of detail between simple architectural forms and richly dressed facades. Buildings need visible depth at street level: recessed doors and windows, frames, sills, cornices, plinths and porches. Add balconies, canopies, external stairs, pipes and rooftop equipment where appropriate to the building type.

Cornices are real volumes with textures. Drawn fine detail can supplement the volume. Use consistent architectural modules and coherent material treatment while allowing enough variation to avoid visibly identical facades everywhere.

Every reachable architectural surface intended for standing, jumping or moving across must have suitable collision. This includes accessible porches, ledges, stairs, fence tops and selected low roofs. Match collision to the usable visible surface; test approach, landing, walking and leaving it. These requirements establish traversable surfaces, not movable building parts.

Provide local opportunities to pass, hide or change height using these elements. The chosen scope is local traversal around buildings, not a continuous network across all balconies and roofs.

Small environmental litter, including cigarette ends in Rough District, may be static textures or decals. Such marks do not need movable physics objects. Their scale, placement and contrast should make sense from the nearby player's viewpoint.

Fill larger empty areas with places that have a purpose: courtyards, gardens, sheds, garages, small parks and service yards. Choose the contents by district. Preserve paths and activities rather than covering unused ground with arbitrary clutter.

## Pilot areas and implementation sequence

Use two complementary pilot areas before extending the approach across the map:

1. **Hamlet and an adjoining road outside the district.** Demonstrate compact plots and house scale, usable terrain, compatible nature, purposeful infill, architectural depth and a clear difference between road travel and off-road cuts.
2. **Rough District.** Demonstrate compact courtyards, garages and service spaces, physical facade detail, small static litter and local traversal on reachable surfaces.

The interview selected both areas. There is no agreed ordering between the two pilots. They must establish a consistent treatment of scale, materials, nature and collision.

For each pilot, first capture existing views and route measurements. Revise layout and terrain, then add buildings, nature and surface detail. Validate physical access throughout rather than discovering blocked routes after dressing is complete.

Review the pilot views with Fedya. Once their treatment is accepted, apply it to the remaining districts and the land connecting them. Check district joins and the entire road network after integrating each area.

Create new numbered revisions for changed map data, models, exports and scenes. Preserve earlier deliverables, existing asset identities and unrelated work. Use the existing project and map pipeline. Update dependent placements and graphs together so the assembled map reflects the revised source data.

## Acceptance checks

Acceptance requires both visual review and physical playtesting. Record the scene and revision, camera or route, van configuration, result and supporting screenshots or measurements for each check.

| Check | Acceptance condition | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human scale | Nearby buildings, doors and architectural elements feel proportionate to the player, NPC and van. Carried NPCs fit through required entrances. | Matched street-level views and carrying tests at changed entrances. |
| Compaction | Previously excessive gaps have been reduced or turned into purposeful places; required circulation and activity spaces still function. | Matched before and after views, dimensions and route measurements. |
| Terrain | Formerly flat problem areas show coherent local shape and spatial divisions within the agreed starting ranges. Objects meet the ground correctly. | Walking views, terrain profiles where needed, and seam inspection. |
| Ordinary van travel | Required roads, accesses, turns and loading areas remain usable without persistent grounding or tipping. | Recorded driving routes and grade checks. |
| Off-road travel | Specified shortcuts remain possible, while obstructed directions are visibly and physically difficult or impassable. Empty-ground cuts are not consistently easier than the road. | Same-configuration road and off-road comparisons, with time and failure causes. |
| Natural collision | Trunks, major rocks and logs block movement coherently with their visible shape; grass and soft shrubs remain traversable. | On-foot and van contact tests at representative obstacles and openings. |
| Views | Ordinary pilot routes have composed views; intentionally broad views have visible landscape content or a chosen subject. | Walking and van camera captures along the reviewed routes, including corners and approaches. |
| Unfinished gaps | The reviewed views do not expose voids, unfinished terrain, cracks, floating objects or missing backs beneath and between visible elements. | Inspection from ordinary routes and all newly reachable raised surfaces. |
| Building collision | Usable architectural surfaces support approach, landing, standing, movement and exit without falling through or colliding with an unrelated shape. | Traversal tests on each changed accessible surface type. |
| Environmental detail | Infill has a district-appropriate purpose; small litter and wear stay subordinate to readable paths, objects and architecture. Nature fits the established visual treatment. | First-person review of both pilots, followed by representative views in each remaining area. |
| Connectivity | Relocated points retain their required access; the road and pedestrian graphs match the new geometry and district joins have no breaks or duplicates. | Updated graph checks, route traversal and assembled-map inspection. |
| Performance | Added detail is assessed on the same scene, machine and settings as the baseline; any regressions are recorded and addressed for the agreed target. | Comparable frame timing and resource measurements, including transitions between areas. |

Sightline estimates and coverage percentages may help find problems, but they cannot replace these checks. No universal sightline distance or percentage reduction has been agreed. Record deliberate broad views and their purpose so a legitimate panorama is not treated as a defect.

## Remaining decisions outside the core specification

The following details remain open and must not be presented as settled:

- Exact vehicle upgrade effects on off-road travel. Validate the map first with the ordinary van, then tune the relevant upgrades when defined.
- Final coordinates, dimensions, object counts and route measurements for each revised area. Establish these through the pilot layout and tests rather than inventing them in advance.
- Specific off-road passage locations and panorama viewpoints. Identify and review them during pilot layout, using the rules above.
- Target hardware and a numeric frame-time or FPS budget. Measure against the existing scene before selecting a production budget.
- Visual decisions already open in ArtDirection.md, including unaccepted palette candidates and unresolved district themes. This specification does not implicitly approve them.

The core scope, allowed layout changes, terrain ranges, nature treatment, collision rules, detail approach and two pilot areas are settled. Their concrete implementation remains subject to the acceptance checks above.
